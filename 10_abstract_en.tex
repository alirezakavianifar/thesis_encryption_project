\chapter*{Abstract}
Image security has become a critical concern in the digital era due to the widespread transmission of visual data over insecure networks. Traditional encryption standards, such as AES and RSA, often struggle with the high data volume and strong pixel correlation inherent in digital images. To address these challenges, this thesis proposes a novel color image encryption algorithm based on a hybrid selection of nine exponential chaotic systems. The proposed method utilizes the three-dimensional Chen chaotic system for initial pixel permutation to break spatial correlations. Subsequently, a dynamic diffusion process is implemented where one of nine distinct exponential chaotic maps is randomly selected for each pixel to modify its value. This multi-layered approach significantly enhances the key space and complexity of the encryption process. Security analyses, including Shannon entropy, correlation coefficients, NPCR, and UACI, demonstrate that the proposed algorithm provides high resistance against various cryptographic attacks while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time applications.

\vspace{1cm}
\textbf{Keywords:} Color Image Encryption, Chaotic Systems, Chen Attractor, Exponential Chaos, Information Security, Cryptanalysis.
